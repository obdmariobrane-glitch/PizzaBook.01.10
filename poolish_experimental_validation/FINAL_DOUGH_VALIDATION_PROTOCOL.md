# Final Dough Validation Protocol

## Purpose

This follow-up tests how selected Poolish samples behave after incorporation into final dough, including whether additional fresh yeast is needed. It is not part of the 81 Poolish sample count. Do not infer remaining yeast activity directly from the original Poolish yeast mass.

## Select Poolish material

After the Poolish study is complete, select conditions based on observed state, not desired recipe outcomes. For each fermentation mode being compared, choose a sample condition that reached the predeclared mature state. Record its full temperature history, yeast dose, maturity evidence, and timepoint. If no sample in a condition is mature within its limit, report that and do not substitute a guessed mature sample.

For a rigorous A/B comparison, prepare separate, independently fermented Poolish batches for A and B; one whole 200 g Poolish cannot be used in two full-size final doughs. Use at least three independent Poolish batches per arm per selected condition. Pair batches by flour/yeast lot and block where possible. Add matched direct controls for each pair. Full planned second-stage coverage is all 27 Poolish matrix conditions (9 RT, 6 CT, 6 RT->CT, and 6 CT->RT), but only conditions that reach the predeclared mature state are eligible. For each eligible condition run A, B, C0, and C+ with three independent replicates per arm. That requires 12 final dough batches per condition: 6 fresh mature Poolish batches for A/B and 6 direct dough controls for C0/C+. If all 27 conditions mature, the second stage contains 324 dough batches; if a condition does not mature, record it as ineligible and do not substitute a different condition. Schedule this substantial follow-up in measured sub-blocks. It is separate from the 81 Poolish samples.

## Reference formula per full-size dough

- Total flour: 300.00 g, comprising 100.00 g Poolish flour + 200.00 g final flour for A/B.
- Total water: 195.00 g, comprising 100.00 g Poolish water + 95.00 g final water for A/B.
- Salt: 6.00 g.
- Poolish: all of one mature sample (100 g flour + 100 g water + its assigned initial yeast).
- Target final dough core temperature: 24.0 +/-0.5 deg C.

Use the same flour lot, water source, salt lot, mixing equipment, mixing schedule, rest, bulk fermentation temperature, container, ball mass, proof temperature, and baking method for all groups. Record actual values, not just settings.

## Comparison arms

For each selected Poolish condition and replicate:

- **A: Poolish, no added yeast.** Incorporate one whole mature Poolish sample; add 200 g flour, 95 g water, and 6 g salt. No additional yeast.
- **B: Poolish + 0.30 g added fresh yeast.** Use a separate matched mature Poolish batch and add 0.30 g fresh yeast to the final dough. If dosed as 10% suspension, add 3.00 g suspension (0.30 g yeast + 2.70 g water) and only 92.30 g additional water so final total remains 195.00 g.
- **C0: direct dough, dose-matched to the original Poolish inoculum.** This is the direct control for A. Use 300 g flour, 195 g total water, 6 g salt, and fresh yeast equal in mass to the Poolish's initial dose (0.10, 0.20, or 0.30 g). Dose via a fresh 10% suspension and subtract its water from 195 g: extra water is 194.10, 193.20, or 192.30 g respectively.
- **C+: direct dough, nominal-mass-matched to B.** This is an additional direct control for B. Use fresh yeast equal to the Poolish initial dose plus 0.30 g: 0.40, 0.50, or 0.60 g for the three starting doses. With 10% suspension, use 4, 5, or 6 g suspension and additional water 191.40, 190.50, or 189.60 g respectively. This controls nominal input mass, not biological activity.

C0 and C+ are both direct-control subarms. Keep them distinct in labels and analysis. If only one direct control is feasible, predeclare whether it matches the original inoculum (C0) or nominal total mass (C+); the omitted comparison remains unanswered.

All formulas above contain exactly 300 g flour, 195 g water, and 6 g salt. For direct doughs all 195 g water is added during mixing, with suspension water deducted as specified. Check arithmetic before weighing.

## Mixing and temperature procedure

1. Bring ingredients and equipment to a consistent starting condition. Choose water temperature by a preliminary mix so all arms finish at 24.0 +/-0.5 deg C; use the same method to adjust it in every arm and record actual water temperature.
2. For A/B, add the Poolish and measured water (including yeast suspension in B), mix until dispersed using the same speed/action and duration. Add flour; mix for the fixed predeclared duration. Add salt at the same stage in every arm and mix to the same endpoint. For C0/C+, combine water/suspension, flour, and salt with the same sequence and schedule; any unavoidable sequence difference must be recorded and repeated consistently.
3. Start the final-dough timer at the end of mixing. Measure dough core temperature immediately with a sanitized probe at the same depth. Record mix start/end, temperature, actual ingredient masses, and deviations.
4. Use identical covered bulk containers. Record dough mass and initial volume/height in a straight-sided graduated vessel or validated fixed geometry. Do not degas one group differently.

## Fermentation, balling, and proof observations

Record at mix-end and at least every 30 minutes during bulk fermentation (every 15 minutes near a suspected endpoint): core temperature, height/volume, bubbles, surface, strength, extensibility, stickiness, and signs of weakening. End bulk at a predeclared objective endpoint applied to all arms (for example a fixed volume increase selected before the run); also report elapsed time. Do not stop each dough at a different subjective maturity and then compare elapsed times as if endpoints matched.

Divide every dough into equal-mass balls using the same target mass and tolerance. Record ball mass, rounding method, seam placement, and any tearing. Rest/proof under one measured temperature and cover. Observe at fixed intervals for ball height/spread or volume, skin drying, bubbles, handling, extensibility, and ability to open without tearing. Record the final proof time and condition before baking.

## Baking and evaluation

Use one oven, one baking surface, one rack position, one preheat protocol, and measured surface temperature. Standardize dough piece/ball mass, opening diameter, sauce and topping masses, bake order, and target bake time or endpoint. Randomize bake order within each block and blind-code the samples for sensory scoring if possible. Allow the same cooling time before crumb assessment.

Record oven and stone/steel temperature, bake duration, launch time, and any recovery between pizzas. Evaluate: opening effort, tearing, extensibility, spring/edge height, spread, bottom/top browning, crumb cell distribution, gumminess, aroma, and flavor. Use a predeclared scale (for example 1-5 with written anchors) and separate objective measurements from sensory notes. Do not taste raw dough.

## Interpretation limits

Compare A with C0 and B with C+ as nominal-input controls, while recognizing that yeast in a fermented Poolish is not biologically equivalent to freshly added yeast. Compare A versus B for the effect of adding 0.30 g fresh yeast to otherwise matched mature Poolish dough. A small final-dough study cannot establish a universal residual-yeast correction for all temperatures, flours, or maturity states. Preserve pair/block identity and report the full distribution, not just the fastest dough.
